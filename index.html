Onboarding Workflow Redesign

60%Ramp reduction
10+Trained
0Failures month 1

The Problem

At Mr. Cooper, new associates joining the foreclosure and compliance team were taking 8–10 weeks to reach independent productivity. During those ramp-up periods, QC scores for the broader team visibly dipped — the team was absorbing errors from trainees who didn't yet have the full picture of exception handling, document validation standards, or escalation logic.

There was no structured onboarding curriculum. Training was ad hoc, largely shadow-based, and inconsistent between cohorts.

"Quality spikes during onboarding cycles disappeared completely."

— Mr. Cooper / Nationstar QC Manager · 2024

What I Built

01
Audit of failure patterns: Reviewed error logs from the previous three onboarding cohorts to identify which task categories produced the most first-month mistakes — exception routing, document type identification, and restricted-state flagging were the main sources.
02
Structured curriculum: Built a week-by-week training plan with clear competency gates. Week 1–2 covered document types and system navigation. Week 3 introduced live case processing with review checkpoints. Week 4 covered exception handling and escalation paths independently.
03
Checklists and reference materials: Created step-by-step verification checklists for each case type, so new hires weren't relying on memory for compliance-critical decisions. These became the team's reference standard.
04
Buddy review protocol: Introduced a structured peer review window in week 3, where trainees processed real cases but had output checked before submission. This identified gaps early without creating downstream errors.

Outcome

Across all cohorts trained under the new program: time to full independent productivity dropped from 8–10 weeks to 3–4 weeks — a 60% reduction. Zero quality failures recorded in any trainee's first month of independent processing. The QC Manager specifically noted that onboarding-cycle quality dips, which had been a recurring pattern, stopped entirely.

3-Layer Fraud Detection Framework

10+Flags/month
0False positives
19moSustained

The Problem

In KYC operations for Rocket Companies, fraudulent accounts and high-risk identity cases weren't always obvious at intake. Forged deeds, identity mismatches, and SSN manipulation could pass a surface check if the reviewer wasn't applying systematic cross-verification — and one missed flag meant the case entered the processing queue and potentially closed without proper scrutiny.

The Framework

L1
Identity Layer: Every case screened at intake for PII consistency — SSN cross-referenced against internal records, government databases, and any supporting identity documents (passport, driver's licence, marriage certificate). Any mismatch flagged for L2 before processing.
L2
Document Layer: Legal documents — Deed of Trust, Promissory Notes, Quit Claim Deeds, Gift Deeds, Warranty Deeds — verified against county records, court verification channels, and government portals. Signature patterns, notary stamps, and recording dates checked for anomalies.
L3
Property Layer: Property type, assessed value, ownership history, and tax records cross-validated using county portals, NetronLine, Zillow, and Realtor.com. Discrepancies between what the customer claimed and what records showed triggered a fraud review.

When a Flag Was Raised

On identifying a high-risk account: conducted thorough internal re-verification, compiled a complete evidence document (reason for flag, supporting data from each layer), coordinated double sign-off with the client, permanently closed the case from the operations queue, blocked it from re-entering, and forwarded it to the internal investigations team. This process ensured zero false-positive escalations — every flagged case had documented evidence before it went to the client.

Outcome

Maintained a consistent 10+ fraud flags per month across the full 19-month engagement, with zero client-escalated false positives. Zero cases missed that were later identified by the client's own team.

18%Throughput increase

The Problem

During the Mr. Cooper foreclosure compliance engagement, I noticed that complex exception cases — those requiring escalation or multi-step review — were regularly causing the same duplication: two team members would begin review on the same case independently because the routing logic for exception queues wasn't clearly defined. This wasted processing time and created version control problems when both reviewers updated records.

The Fix

01
Mapping the exception flow: Documented how exception cases were currently routed — from identification through to resolution — and marked every point where duplicated effort was occurring. Found three recurring handoff failures in the queue management process.
02
Standardised routing rules: Defined clear ownership logic for each exception category. Each case type got a single designated reviewer role, with a defined handoff point if escalation was needed. Ambiguity in who owned a case was removed.
03
Documented in SOP: Formalised the new routing logic into a team SOP so it wasn't dependent on individuals knowing the convention — it became the written standard, reviewable by anyone.

Outcome

Team throughput increased 18% within the engagement period — measured against the same case volume, same headcount, same error rate targets. The gain came purely from eliminating duplicated effort on exception cases. No quality trade-off was made to achieve the output increase.

Note on baseline: the 18% figure reflects the improvement in team throughput relative to the pre-change baseline measured during the same engagement period. I am prepared to discuss the specific measurement methodology in interview.

Why I Built It

When I joined the Rocket Companies KYC team, there was no centralised procedure document for the specific case types we handled — death notifications, ownership transfers, SSN corrections, VA loan compliance. Team members were relying on informal knowledge and varied approaches. With 45+ cases per day in a compliance-sensitive environment, that inconsistency was a liability.

What the SOP Covered

01
Identity verification protocol: Step-by-step guide for authenticating SSN, driver's licence, passport, and marriage certificates — including cross-reference sources (county records, government portals) and specific check criteria for each document type.
02
Document authentication checklist: Case-type-specific checklists for every document category handled — mortgage deeds, probate records, transfer deeds, VA certificates — with required verification steps, red flags, and escalation triggers.
03
Fraud flag procedure: Documented the full 3-layer flag-to-closure process, including evidence compilation requirements, client sign-off protocol, and investigation team handoff steps.
04
Restricted-state routing: Clear procedure for identifying compliance-restricted states (Washington, West Virginia, Nevada) during case processing and routing immediately to the dedicated compliance team — preventing processing violations.

Impact

The SOP became the team's standard reference for all KYC and mortgage account processing. All 4 analysts I onboarded were trained using this document — reaching full independent productivity within 30 days with zero quality failures. It also served as the source of truth during the fraud flag process, ensuring consistent evidence compilation across every high-risk case.
