Case Studies: Fraud Detection

Catching What the System Already Cleared

Why I Caught Cases Others Missed

Most mortgage operations professionals verify what is in front of them — a document is present, a signature exists, a field is filled. That is surface-level verification. It catches simple errors. It does not catch fraud.

My standard was different: every case went through three layers of verification regardless of whether a flag had been raised. Fraud is specifically designed to appear clean on the surface. The only way to know is to go deeper.

Full-Field Review

  • Every field verified against its corresponding source document
  • Not just presence, but accuracy against county and government records
  • Cross-reference all identifying information with authoritative sources
↓

Multi-Source Cross-Check

  • Cross-referenced against at least two independent external sources simultaneously
  • A discrepancy in one source = data entry error
  • A discrepancy across multiple sources = flag for investigation
↓

Pattern Recognition

  • Compared each case against fraud patterns documented previously
  • Forged documents have consistent tells — formatting inconsistencies, name mismatches, notarisation anomalies
  • Historical case similarity analysis to identify recurring fraud attempts

Result: 10+ fraud cases caught monthly that had passed system validation

False Positive Rate: 0% across 19 months

Three Real Cases from Rocket Companies

Case 01: Forged Quit Claim Deed — Grantor Name Mismatch

Document Forgery
System: Cleared
Outcome: Fraud Confirmed

Background

A routine ownership transfer request arrived in my queue — a Quit Claim Deed submitted to change the property owner on record. The system had processed it through standard validation and returned no flags. It was assigned to me for KYC review.

What I Found

During my Layer 01 full-field review, the grantor name on the submitted Quit Claim Deed did not match the name on record in the county recorder database for the same property.

The discrepancy was one character off in the last name — close enough to pass an automated check, not close enough to be a legitimate match when cross-referenced against three independent county sources.

Why It Was Missed

The system's validation checks verified that the document was present, properly formatted, and internally consistent. They were not designed to cross-reference the grantor name against external county recorder data — that step was manual.

The fraud was in the identity of the grantor, not the structure of the document.

What I Did

  • Stopped processing immediately
  • Compiled a full evidence document detailing every point of discrepancy with county record references
  • Coordinated joint review and double sign-off with the Rocket Companies client
  • Permanently closed the case, blocked it from re-entering the processing queue
  • Forwarded to the internal investigations team

Case 02: Borrower Identity Mismatch Across Systems

Identity Fraud
System: Pre-cleared at Intake
Outcome: Fraud Confirmed

Background

A sensitive account update case arrived — the type requiring identity verification before any action. Intake had confirmed that the customer contact details matched what was on file. The case was forwarded to me as ready for processing.

What I Found

My full CDD screen cross-referenced SSN, driver's licence details, and government records simultaneously.

The SSN on the submitted identity document did not reconcile correctly across all three sources. In the mortgage servicing system it was associated with one identity profile. Cross-referenced against government verification channels, it returned a different name variant that matched neither the contact name nor the submitted ID document.

No single system had caught this because each only compared within its own data.

Why It Was Missed

The intake check verified that the contact name matched what was on the account. It had not performed multi-source cross-verification of the SSN against external government records.

The mismatch only existed at the intersection of internal and external data — a gap only a manual CDD process surfaces.

What I Did

  • Processing stopped immediately
  • Documented the SSN discrepancy in full — all conflicting data from each source with references
  • Compiled evidence package
  • Coordinated client sign-off
  • Permanently closed and blocked the case
  • Forwarded to investigations

Case 03: Customer A Documents Attached to Customer B Account

Cross-Customer Document Error / Fraud Attempt
Prior QC: Passed
Outcome: Blocked

Background

A case arrived that had already passed a prior processing stage and quality check. Routine account update, supporting documentation attached, nothing in the case summary indicating a problem.

What I Found

During my end-to-end case review, the supporting documents attached to the account did not belong to the account holder on record.

The identity documents, property records, and supporting materials corresponded to a completely different customer's account. Names, property details, and account references in the attached documents did not match the account they had been filed against.

Why It Was Missed

The prior QC check had verified that documents were present and that document types matched what was required. It had not performed a full content cross-check — specifically borrower names, property addresses, and account references — against the account they were attached to.

That level of content verification was not part of the standard prior-stage check.

What I Did

  • Halted processing and escalated immediately to the Team Lead and directly to the Rocket client
  • Recognized that cross-customer document errors have legal implications for both affected accounts
  • Documented the discrepancy in full
  • Identified which accounts were impacted
  • Coordinated remediation for both accounts
  • Case blocked, both accounts reviewed, forwarded to investigations
