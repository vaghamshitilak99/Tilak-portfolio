Purpose & Scope

Establishes the mandatory framework for conducting KYC verification and Customer Due Diligence for mortgage account modifications processed on behalf of Rocket Companies. Authored by Tilak Vaghamshi with approvals from Operations Manager, Team Lead, Compliance Officer, and Rocket Companies client representative.

Regulatory Compliance Covered
  • Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) — Core AML/KYC compliance requirement for all account modifications
  • USA PATRIOT Act Section 326 — Customer Identification Program (CIP) requirements
  • OFAC Regulations — Sanctions screening and prohibited entity identification
  • Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) — Consumer data access and verification protocols
  • RESPA / TILA / CFPB — Mortgage-specific disclosure and servicing requirements
  • Rocket Companies Internal Policies — Client-specific compliance overlay requirements
Case Types Covered by This SOP
  • SSN Corrections — Full KYC + CDD required. Cross-verify against government records before any update.
  • Name Changes — Full KYC + CDD. Verify legal documentation (marriage certificate, court order, divorce decree).
  • Ownership Transfers — Full KYC + CDD + Title Review. Deed verification, probate records, inheritance documentation.
  • Death Notifications — Full KYC + Enhanced Due Diligence. Death certificate verification, executor identity, probate filing cross-check.
  • Address Changes — Standard KYC. County and government record cross-reference required.
  • Contact Information Updates — Standard KYC. Identity confirmation before any modification.
Multi-Source Verification Methodology
  • Layer 1 — Internal System Verification — Cross-reference submitted documents against all active internal mortgage servicing records (LSAMS, Black Knight MSP, Dynamics 365)
  • Layer 2 — Government & Court Record Verification — Authenticate documents against county deed records, court filings, probate records, and government identity databases
  • Layer 3 — Third-Party Cross-Validation — Validate property-related information through county search portals, NetronLine, Zillow, and Realtor.com
  • Red Flag Escalation — Any discrepancy across layers triggers the full fraud investigation protocol: re-verification, evidence documentation, client double-sign-off, case closure, and forwarding to investigations

Problem Statement

The Mr. Cooper Foreclosure & Compliance team had a QC rejection rate averaging 7–8% monthly — above the industry benchmark of <5%. Without systematic error tracking, the team was repeatedly fixing the same types of mistakes with no root-cause understanding. 1,247 rejections over 6 months translated to approximately 415 hours of rework.

Solution

Built an Excel-based Error Trend Analysis Dashboard (Pivot Tables, Power Query, VBA Macros) that automated data collection from QC logs and visualized error patterns over 6 months across a team processing 12,500+ documents per month.

Top 3 Root Causes (70% of All Rework)
  • Cross-Customer Document Errors — Documents filed under incorrect borrower accounts. Root cause: insufficient pre-filing verification step.
  • Missing Notarizations — Required notarial acknowledgements absent from submitted documents. Root cause: checklist not mandating notarization verification at intake.
  • Incomplete Court Filings — Foreclosure filings missing mandatory court-required attachments. Root cause: state-specific filing requirements not consistently applied.
Intervention & Results

The Problem This Framework Solves

In mortgage account servicing, customer-initiated requests (SSN corrections, name changes, ownership transfers, death notifications) create fraud opportunities. Traditional operations rely on automated system checks and single-source verification — which fail because fraudulent documents are designed to pass automated checks, and internal systems only know what was previously submitted (which could also be fraudulent).

Framework Architecture: The 3 Layers
  • Layer 1 — Full-Field Review — Every field of every submitted document is verified against internal records. Document completeness check. Internal consistency validation. Any discrepancy triggers Layer 2. Clean cases proceed to processing.
  • Layer 2 — External Cross-Verification — County records, government databases, court filings verified against submitted documents. Any mismatch triggers Layer 3. Matches with Layer 1 proceed with additional monitoring.
  • Layer 3 — Pattern Matching — Behavioral pattern analysis against known fraud signatures. Cross-case comparison to identify coordinated fraud attempts. At minimum 3 independently verifiable discrepancies required before escalation.
Evidence Documentation Standard

The zero false positive rate was achieved through a disciplined evidence threshold: no case was escalated without at least three independently verifiable discrepancies documented across multiple data sources.

Escalation Protocol
  • Internal double-verification of all flagged evidence
  • Full supporting evidence document compiled detailing every discrepancy
  • Joint client sign-off required before any action taken
  • Case permanently closed from operations end — blocked from entering processing queue
  • Forwarding to internal investigations team for legal or law enforcement action
Fraud Types Detected
  • Ownership Transfer Fraud — Forged deeds attempting to redirect property rights to unauthorized parties
  • Identity Assumption — Fraudulent SSN correction attempts to assume borrower identity
  • Death Certificate Fraud — Forged death certificates to trigger insurance payouts or estate transfers
  • Account Takeover — Unauthorized contact information changes to intercept sensitive communications
  • Document Tampering — Altered legal documents including Quit Claim Deeds, Gift Deeds, and Warranty Deeds
Performance Metrics
  • 120+ verified fraud detections over 19 months — approximately 6–7 per month sustained
  • Zero false positives — Zero legitimate cases incorrectly escalated as fraudulent
  • Zero false negatives (client-escalated) — Zero fraud cases that were processed and later discovered by the client
  • Contributed to 3 TCS awards including specific recognition for fraud detection performance
